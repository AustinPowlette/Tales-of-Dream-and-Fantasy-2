# Story Plan: chapters for the ten Story starts

> **Status (2026-10-10): implemented** (`Chapters`, `ChapText`, `GuiChapter`, `Tale*`, `StoryCodas`). Probe: `tools/probes/TalesProbe.java`.

Before this, each Story start was a flat list of four objectives ("reach Major Lvl 2", "make 25 items", "defeat Cinder Wraith") with no scenes, and the enemy was an ordinary NPC frozen in Originis. Now each start is a short **novel in chapters**, with the people of the story living in the world, choices that matter, and an ending written for each way the Garden can end.

## How a chapter works

A chapter is: an **opening scene** (a window; people talk; sometimes a question), a **task** (one thing to do; it is in the Quest Log as `Story: ...`), and a **closing scene** when the task is done, often with a second question. Then a reward, and the next chapter waits a while (years, not weeks) so life goes on between chapters.

- **Tasks:** reach a realm (`REALM`), make N items (`CRAFT`, or "N since the chapter began" with `CHECK`), hold N stones (`WEALTH`), N friends, N kills, open N shops, found a sect / a city, tame a beast, reach an age, go to a place (`PLACE`), meet a person (`MEET`), beat a person (`DEFEAT`), wait N weeks (`WAIT`), or nothing (`NONE`: a scene only).
- **Gates:** a chapter can wait for a gap since the last one, an age, a Major level, or any condition of the story's flags. A chapter that belongs to another road can be **skipped** (`skipIf`).
- **People:** each story has a cast. A person is created (a real `Character`, a level or more above the human, with potential to stay there) when a chapter needs them, in a city / sect / empty land the story picks. They are **fated** to the human (`Character.fatedTo`): only that human can kill them (old age, the epoch, plague and everyone else leave them alone; a stranger who beats one leaves them hurt). A foe who beats a hero on the Story difficulty leaves them alive and hurt (on Hard and Brutal the usual death roll applies). When the story ends, or a person nears old age, they become ordinary again.
- **Places:** `place(key, selector)`: `far` / `near` (a city), `sect`, `wild` / `wildnear` (empty land; made if the world has none yet), `dungeon`, `start` (Originis). Picked when first needed; different places are different tiles.
- **Choices:** a question after a scene; the answer is a **flag** (option number, 1-based). Later lines may test flags with a prefix: `?flag|` (set), `?!flag|` (not set), `?flag=2|` (equals). The automatic flags `dead.<person>` and `beaten.<person>` tell what happened to a person of the story. A flag can also be rolled at the start (`begin`) for a hidden truth that changes from game to game.
- **Episodes:** a pool of short tales that come up by chance while you travel (the Orphan's road tales, a few memories of the Disciple's master), at most N, so many years apart, each only once, only where their condition holds (in a town, in the wilds).
- **Epilogue:** a closing scene made from the flags. **Codas** (`StoryCodas`): the last lines of the Palace's ending, one for each start and each of five families (stay, soldier, leave, fall, world); one credits line says whether the story was finished.
- Text: `* ` is narration, `@Name| words` is speech (`@You|` is the player), `{name} {first} {last} {year} {age}`, `{key}` / `{key.first}` / `{key.last}` a person, `{@place}` a place. A line of text is plain words, about eight to nine words to a sentence (the probe measures it).
- Old saves keep their flat list of objectives (`Starts.State.v2` is false); new lives get chapters.
- Typed: `story` (the chapters: done, current with its task, the rest as ???), `story replay [id]` (read a scene again). The Story window has a list and a Read-again button.

## The ten stories

The truth of each story, in the order it is found. Names in brackets are people of the cast.

1. **The Last Heir of the Azure Cloud Sect** (12 chapters). The hand that burned the sect was Cinder Wraith, raised in Marrow Reach; the man who paid him was the kind Magistrate Orrin Vale; the reason was the sect's Silent Tonic, made of a root that Orrin's agents dug out of Marrow Reach until the water killed the village. Master Hui learned it, refused another batch and wrote that he would tell the Court; Orrin burned the sect a week before. [Wen Sorrel, Orrin Vale, Cinder Wraith, Pell Wraith]. Choices: mercy (Marrow Reach), patron (Orrin's money), kill or spare Cinder, how to end Orrin (expose / duel / take his offer), what the cauldron is for. If Cinder is killed, his ward Pell comes for revenge twenty years later and may have killed Wen first; if he is spared, he writes from the south.
2. **The Rich Scion** (9 chapters). Someone in the household sells the house's caravan routes to House Kessel. The traitor is rolled at the start: the steward Doric Penn, the cousin Ysolde Rook or the bodyguard Gareth Ironwall. All three have a human secret that looks like guilt; the night of the false road settles it. [Doric, Ysolde, Gareth, Odile Kessel]. Choices: the first answer to the ambush, Kessel's offer, trusting each suspect, the traitor's fate, how the trade war ends. The father's sealed letter is read when the city is founded.
3. **The Orphan of the Ash Road** (3 chapters + 15 road tales). Old Brannoch gives a brass token with a seven-pointed star and dies; five of the tales (a ferryman, a seamstress, a dying soldier, an archivist, an old maid) recognise it; when three are found the last chapter opens: the lantern shrine and a mother who has lit it every night. A love story (Sol) runs through three tales. [Brannoch, Kasumi].
4. **The Disgraced Disciple** (10 chapters). Half of the Scroll of Unbroken Breath was in your desk; the other half is in four seals hidden by Elder Zhao (a drowned library, a silent monk, a rival's vault, and Mei, your friend). Why your desk is rolled at the start: Zhao put it there to silence a question about the Pillar Fund, or a dying friend, Lan, hid it there, or the scroll found you. The technique grows (its name changes) with each seal. The scroll is good; Zhao feared it because a student who can mend and stand alone needs no master. [Mei Lingfeng, Brother Tse, Elder Zhao].
5. **The Blacksmith's Child** (8 chapters). The father's lessons return as you work the forge. A war between the Northern League and the Marsh Dukes orders arms from you (League / Dukes / both in secret / neither); behind the forge wall is his unfinished sword Evenfall and a letter: he refused to make weeping blades for Quartermaster Voss, and Voss's men killed him. The sword cannot cut anyone who has not raised a hand. [Pia Fenn, Voss].
6. **The Beast-Tamer's Daughter** (7 chapters). The mother, Isha Wildmere, walked into the mountains with three great companions: Moss the lake tortoise, Quill the storm crane, Old Ember the cinder boar. They are found one by one and become real companions (`Taming.legend`, whatever the cap). Ember was burned by hunters and fears fire; Captain Brodin is after him. Isha died getting him out. [Brodin].
7. **The Ghost-Touched** (9 chapters). Three witnesses give three absurd accounts of your death (a bun, a crab, a poem); the truth is a peach pit, a goose and a bridge. Then the dead come, one at a time, with small unfinished things, and Old Meng tells you that you are not touched but a door. [Old Meng, Dumpling Lu, Captain Foo, Lin Zhaoyu, Bao Tian].
8. **The Lucky Fool** (6 chapters). A string of near-disasters you walk out of (a wrong hat, a tournament won by default, a challenger who slips on soap). A small Luck Spirit, Mrs. Fortune, was assigned to you by a clerical error and retires; the last peril has no luck in it, and your friends are what save you. [Pip Tallow, Master Gu].
9. **The Last of the Silver Crane Clan** (8 chapters). The clan guarded Stillspring, a spirit vein under its hall; the uncle Aldous signed it away to Baron Hexley. One of the three surviving cousins knew and kept quiet (rolled at the start: Tamsin, Joren or Isla). [Tamsin, Joren, Isla, Baron Hexley].
10. **The Wandering Immortal's Disciple** (6 chapters + 3 tales). Three moves; each comes back with one answer to "what is your name" (the moon, the road, the gourd). The master does not belong to the Garden and the story never says where he is from. Only small things are wrong about him. His name is Wen Jiu ("it isn't the first one"). Nothing explains the Palace.

## Adding or changing a story

One file per story (`TaleXxx.java`): `Chapters.def(id)`, `.cast(...)`, `.place(...)`, `.chapter(...)...`, `.epilogue(...)`, plus its five codas in `StoryCodas`, then `register()` it in `Chapters`' static block. `TalesProbe` lints every story (conditions, placeholders, sentence length), plays each one to the end on 12 random roads and checks the words that were shown.
