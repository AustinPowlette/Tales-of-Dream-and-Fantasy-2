# Lessons, favors and little stories: all the text

Placeholders: `{You}` is how the speaker addresses you (your name, "friend", "young one"...), `{Name}` your name, `{me}` the speaker's name. A lesson is told in the **warm** voice by cheerful, warm and scholarly speakers and in the **dry** voice by the others; after it you may ask "Tell me more".

## Lessons (21)

Each is told once per person, when someone stops to talk to you (or you talk first) and the situation is true; at least 6 weeks apart.

### 1. The slow fire

*Teaches:* You cultivate every week by yourself; meditating and spirit veins add more.

**Warm voice**

> {You}, sit a moment. You walk like someone who is late for something.
>
> I was the same. An old woman cured me with one sentence: you don't chase qi, qi finds you.
>
> Every week your body cultivates on its own. You don't have to do a thing for that.
>
> Meditating adds a little extra. A spirit vein under your feet adds more, every single week.
>
> So a quiet week is not a wasted week. Remember that when the road feels slow.
>

**Dry voice**

> You. Yes, you. Stop looking at the road like it owes you money.
>
> Cultivation happens every week. Walking, trading, sleeping, it makes no difference. That part is free.
>
> Meditating is the bonus. Spirit veins are the jackpot. Stand on one and you gain extra qi every week.
>
> Veins are rare. Learn where they are. Or don't, and stay slow. Your choice.
>

**"Tell me more"**

> Your Stats sheet has a bar. That bar is your cultivation.
>
> When it fills at the last level of a realm, you can try to break through.
>
> Until then there is no shame in simply living.
>

**"Tell me more"**

## Favors

A conversation can end with a request (a place within 14 tiles with wild beasts, not too far above your strength). The speaker opens with one of three lines, then names the pay; you answer.

**Openings** (one of three, `where` is "here" or "at <place> (<coordinates>)"; sometimes a short greeting from the speaker's voice comes first)

> Could I ask a favor? Beasts have been getting bold <where>. They've scared off every honest worker in the area.
>
> You look like someone who can handle a fight. There are beasts <where> that need thinning out. Could you do it?
>
> Forgive me for asking a stranger's help. Beasts are ruining the work <where>. I'd feel safer if someone strong cleared them.
>
> I can pay <N> ss when it's done.

**Your answers:** *I'll do it.* / *I can't, sorry.* / *Not now.*

**If you accept** (one of): "Thank you. Truly. Come find me when it's done." / "You have my thanks. Be careful out there." / "Heaven watch over you. And thank you." Then: *A new quest is in your Quest Log: Thin out the beasts for <name>.* When it is done: *<name> thanks you for the favor and pays <N> ss.*

**If you refuse** (one of): "I understand. It was a lot to ask." / "No harm in asking. Another time, perhaps."  **If you say not now:** "Of course. If you change your mind, I'll still be here."

## Little stories (60)

Lines starting with "*" are narration; `Name:` is somebody speaking. The text after each choice is what you read once you have chosen. Each story is told once per person, about one turn in 20 when one fits, 20 weeks apart.

### 1. The riddle at the bridge

*Told in: Qi Cultivation to Foundation*

*A man sits on the rail of a small stone bridge, swinging his boots over the water.*

**Bridge keeper:** The toll today is a riddle. Coins are boring.

**Bridge keeper:** I have cities, but no houses. Mountains, but no trees. Water, but no fish. What am I?

**What do you say?**

1. "A map."
2. "A dream."
3. Pay two spirit stones and skip it.  *(the default if you close the window)*

*What follows each choice* (text in brackets is what the game changes):

- **"A map."**

  **Bridge keeper:** A map! Nobody gets it. Not once in three years.

  *He tosses you a small purse. "Forty stones. Don't spend them all in one place." (+40 ss)*

- **"A dream."**

  **Bridge keeper:** A dream. Wrong, but pretty. Go on, then.

  *He waves you across without a toll. You decide he likes you a little.*

- **Pay two spirit stones and skip it.**

  *Ending a:*

  **Bridge keeper:** Cheap or clever? I can never tell.

  *You cross with a lighter purse and a heavier mind. (-2 ss)*

  *Ending b:*

  **Bridge keeper:** No stones, no riddle? Fine. Cross. I'm too tired to argue.

*A clerk in a counting-house unrolls a yellow note. Your name is on it, in your own hand, for 26,000 stones. It is ninety years old.*

**Clerk:** The lender is long dead. The house kept the note. Interest at three in the hundred.

**Clerk:** You do not have to pay. But the house would remember.

**What do you do?**

1. Pay it (26,000 ss).  *(the default if you close the window)*
2. Argue that the interest is unfair.
3. Say it is not your hand.

*What follows each choice* (text in brackets is what the game changes):

- **Pay it (26,000 ss).**

  *Ending a:*

  *He stamps it, folds it, and holds it out to you. You keep it. It is odd to hold a promise you made so long ago.*

  *(-26,000 ss, more cultivation, a calmer mind)*

  *Ending b:*

  **Clerk:** It is your hand. I have your signature on a hundred other notes.

  *You laugh in spite of yourself and change the subject.*

- **Argue that the interest is unfair.**

  *Ending a:*

  **Clerk:** The house is firm on the rate.

  *You leave with your stones and a feeling that you have lost something larger.*

  *Ending b:*

  **Clerk:** Hm. The house will settle for 6,600.

  *(-6,600 ss, a little more cultivation)*

- **Say it is not your hand.**

  **Clerk:** It is your hand. I have your signature on a hundred other notes.

  *You laugh in spite of yourself and change the subject.*
