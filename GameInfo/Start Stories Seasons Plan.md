## 1. How a Season 1 chapter is triggered today

A chapter has three separate requirements. People often mix them up, so here they are apart.

1. **When it opens ("Starts when")**: all of these must be true. (a) The previous chapter is finished. (b) A **gap** of game time has passed since the previous one ended (0 to 3 years; one chapter in the Heir waits 25 years, one 15). (c) Sometimes an **age** or a **Major level** (the realm: Major 0 = Qi Cultivation, 1 = Foundation, 2 = Core Formation, 3 = Soul Wandering, 4 = Nascent Soul... 7 = Ascension, the Door). (d) Sometimes a **custom condition** (a flag set by an earlier choice, e.g. "three clues found"). A chapter can also be **skipped** altogether if the story took another road.
2. **The task**: what the player must do before the chapter ends. It sits in the Quest Log as `Story: ...`. Kinds in use: *meet a person*, *go to a place*, *defeat a person*, *reach a Major level*, *make N items since the chapter began*, *hold N spirit stones*, *have N friends / kills / shops*, *tame a beast*, *found a sect / city*, or **none** (a "wait" chapter whose closing scene just follows after N weeks, or at once).
3. **The reward**: spirit stones, potential, pills, gear a Major above you, a technique insight.

**Road tales** (episodes) are different: they are not in the line. They come up at random (about 1 turn in 12-30 once their condition holds, at most one every 2-3 years, a fixed total per story), and need only that you are in a town or in the wilds and a few chapters have passed.

### What the tasks of the 79 chapters are

| Task | Chapters | Share |
|---|---|---|
| Go and meet a person | 17 | 22% |
| Go to a place | 16 | 20% |
| Other counts (friends, kills, tame, age, found a sect or city, own shops) | 12 | 15% |
| Reach a Major level | 9 | 11% |
| Defeat a person | 8 | 10% |
| No task (a wait, or the scene follows at once) | 7 | 9% |
| **Make N items** | **6** (five in the Smith: 10, 25, 50, 30, 100; one in the Heir: 10) | 8% |
| Hold N spirit stones | 4 | 5% |

So the answer to "is it craft N items, or waiting for the right time?": for most stories it is **walking to a person or a place** after a **wait of 1-3 game years and often a Major level**. Only the Blacksmith's Child is built on counted crafting.

## 3. Revamping the Tamer and the Smith

### 3.1 The Beast-Tamer's Daughter: make the beasts people

Design goal: by the end of Season 1 the player would be upset if Moss, Quill, Ember or the fox died.

1. **The fox stays.** It is the beast that answered the tune in chapter 1. Name it (the player names it at the end of chapter 1: a cost-free choice that creates ownership). It is a *real* companion (`Taming`), appears in every chapter's opening or closing scene doing one small thing, and is the comic voice. The three great beasts become the *elders* it looks up to.
2. **Each great beast has a wish, not just a location.** Moss wants to see the sea once before he sleeps; Quill wants somebody to catch (she has never been caught); Ember wants to eat an apple from an unburned tree. The wish is the chapter's task: a coast tile, a fall from a cliff in a real fight, a planted orchard. They are small, concrete, cheap in play time, and they pay off in the sanctuary chapter (the orchard, the sea-shell on the lake shore).
3. **Meet them before you get them.** Three short "road tales" before each reunion: you find a stone placed on a path, a feather on your pillow, burnt apples, so the beast has been *watching you* for chapters. The reunion is then a meeting, not an introduction. (Uses the existing episode system.)
4. **They are in the room.** From chapter 3 on, the story reads which companions are alive and with you (`Taming.peek`) and writes the scene around them (a line from whoever is present; a different line if one is missing). If a legend dies in the game, a wake scene plays and the epilogue changes. The sanctuary choice (tamer / guardian / voice) now changes more than one epilogue line.
5. **A real antagonist with a face.** Brodin appears in chapter 2 or 3 as a bounty-hunter the player argues with in a tavern, not as a name on a quest. He has a want (the bounty pays for his daughter's cure; Ember killed eleven people because of fire, and Brodin is the father of one of them).
6. **Mode and voice:** pastoral elegy with comic relief (the fox); voice card C with longer sentences and one cozy image per scene at most.
7. **Length:** 7 chapters → about 10, adding the fox naming, two meet-before-you-get-them tales, and the Brodin tavern; about 4,000 words (from 2,289).

### 3.2 The Blacksmith's Child: replace the grind with commissions, give Pia a past

Design goal: every counted craft belongs to a *person*; the war shows up in the world; Pia is the heart.

1. **Commissions, not counts.** Replace "make 25 items since this chapter began" with *named commissions*: the story tracks what the player crafts and asks for **three to five specific pieces** in a chapter (a hoe for the farmer; a pair of hinges for the widow Marl's gate; a knife for the boy who will not say why he needs it). Each delivered piece triggers a **30-80 word micro-scene** (the customer's reply, a coin, a lie). Total: about 15 pieces over the story instead of 215 items. Any craft counts (the player's trade or amateur), so a Smith who is also a Hunter is not locked.
2. **Pia's past is the mystery.** She did not come to the forge by chance. She was an apprentice in Quartermaster Voss's forge and saw the "weeping blades" made; she ran because Harl was the only smith who had said no three times. She tells nobody. Chapter 4 (the orders) has her go quiet when the Dukes' letter arrives; chapter 5 (the wall) is the moment she tells the player. The player's choice about the war now has a witness who will not forgive one answer.
3. **Pia wants a small thing:** to make *one thing that is hers* (a pair of tongs with her mark). It is a commission. Her mark ends up on the last chapter's sword.
4. **A boy carries your spearhead.** The Greywater scene ("a boy of sixteen is carried home with one of your spearheads in his hands") becomes a character: Tam. He comes to the forge in chapter 7 asking to learn the trade (if you armed the League) or to curse you (if you armed the Dukes) or to buy a hinge (if neither). He is the proof that the war reaches the forge.
5. **Voss earlier.** The grey-coat man in chapter 3's flashback appears in the present as a customer in chapter 2 or 4, polite, buys a hinge, asks for the old smith. The reader sees him before the player knows.
6. **Plant the clock early.** "A tiny ticking, like a clock in a very small house" is the best line in the story and appears in chapter 1 and at the very end. Add it in the middle too, changed (it stops; it runs fast).
7. **Mode and voice:** war drama and workplace warmth; voice card B (routine, onomatopoeia: the hammer's tick-tick, understatement, a worker's humour) plus a little of card A's banter between Pia and the player.
8. **Pacing fix:** commissions are 1-3 weeks of game time each; the story can be finished by year 10 instead of never.

### 3.3 Notes for the other starts (S1 only; small)
- **Lucky Fool:** fix survivability (higher potential floor, or a "lucky" save in the first deaths), keep the voice. Candidate to test card A.
- **Disciple:** leave almost entirely; add one more road tale of a different kind and a mid-story chapter where something *goes wrong*. It is short on purpose.
- **Heir / Disgraced / Scion:** shorten the longest waits (drop or soften the Major gates) and show in the Quest Log what each is waiting for.
- **Orphan:** guarantee the third clue after N years so the last chapter cannot stall.
- **Silver Crane:** make the clan visible (the survivors are real recruits, not names), make the vein audible (a hum that returns).
- **Ghost:** keep as is until Season 2 builds on it.

## 4. The Season engine (technical sketch)

What we have today: `Chapters.Def` holds a list of chapters, a list of episodes, one epilogue, and the story ends (`st.done`). The codas (`StoryCodas`) that follow the Palace's ending already read `st.done`.

| Piece | Change |
|---|---|
| **Data** | `Def` gets a list of `Season` (id, title, gate, chapters, episodes, interlude, epilogue, mode/voice tags). Season 1 is today's Def, unchanged. |
| **State** | `Starts.State` gets `season` (0-based). `chapter` indexes into the current season. Old saves: `season = 0`; a finished S1 stays finished unless the author wants a "Season 2 awakens" option for existing characters. |
| **Interlude vs epilogue** | Today's epilogue says "you finish the story", old age, decades. Split it: an **interlude** (a short scene, "some years later") plays when a season ends and more are waiting; the **epilogue** plays only after the last season. The existing epilogue text can become the last season's. |
| **Gate** | A season opens when the previous is done **and** a gap of N years has passed **and** a soft condition holds. *Do not gate by Major level above what a typical person reaches*: Major 4 is about 51 years away. Gate by years, places, counts that scale with the player (shops, friends, sect members, city population), and events. |
| **Foes** | Already "a level or more above the human" (`Cast.lvl`), so fated foes scale with the player. Keep. |
| **Quest Log** | Show `Story: Season 2, waits for year 34` or `...needs a sect of five`. |
| **Text checks** | `TalesProbe` extends over seasons (every scene reachable, flags exist, placeholders resolve); `StoryDump` prints seasons. |
| **Mechanical echoes** | Each season declares its hooks in the beat sheet (section 6). A small `Echo` helper that reads the real world (companions alive, items crafted by type, dead people the player knew) keeps the text code short. |
| **Cost** | Engine: small (a few hundred lines, no new systems). Writing: the real cost, 3,000-5,000 words per season per story. |

**Pacing budget** (a rule for all new chapters): no chapter waits more than **6 years** after the previous; gap plus task should average **2-4 years**; a season is 6-10 chapters, about 20-30 years of play; Season 2 begins about 3-6 years after Season 1 ends.

**The Door and the Palace:** the Door at Ascension is Major 7, which no NPC reaches in 150 years and a person reaches only very late. Season 3 should therefore **not require the Door**. It can *approach* it (the lore is the background of every final season), and the existing codas (`StoryCodas`, five endings) stay as the optional last page if the player ever gets there.
